1. Product vision
A modern web app that shows e-commerce sellers their true profit after product cost, packaging, 
shipping, ads, platform fees and returns, with a Margin Health Score (0–100) and specific, actionable 
recommendations. It launches internationally for sellers in the UK, EU, US, Canada and Australia, 
through paid monthly or annual subscriptions. There is no Free plan; every subscription tier includes a 
14-day free trial (K38).
2. Positioning
“Find where your margin leaks and what each leak costs you. Plans from £39 a month, 
including VAT. Cancel any time.”
• Margins365 diagnoses rather than only tracking: it ranks margin leaks by impact in the user's 
currency and tells the seller what to fix. 
• Its differentiator is CSV diagnosis without a live store connection, clear “Show the maths” 
explanations, and ranked fixes ahead of dense reporting. TikTok Excel import is coming soon 
(K53). 
• Every paid tier includes true profit, Health Score and confidence, product verdicts, all six 
launch leaks with monthly impact, target prices, calculators, sample data, privacy CSV export, 
email support and the same security protections. Public TikTok copy is “TikTok Shop UK (Beta): 
Excel import coming soon” until the K53 restore gate. 
• Hard monthly order limits and no per-order surprise charges are explicit. At the limit, the 
customer upgrades to analyse more orders that billing month; existing results remain visible. 
• The £39/£59 lifetime offer, “One price, yours for life”, the first-200 early-bird and its counter 
are retired. Any lifetime licence already sold is honoured as a permanent entitlement and is 
not converted into a subscription. 
• Until the TikTok reader passes its tests, public pages use “TikTok Shop UK (Beta): Excel 
import coming soon” (K53). They do not say TikTok upload works now, do not give TikTok 
export instructions, and do not imply TikTok US or EU support.

1. How do I use this platform?
Start a 14-day free trial and choose a plan. Add your shop and pick the platform it uses (Shopify, 
Amazon or Etsy). Download the reports you already get from that platform and upload them — we 
never connect to your store. Add your product costs (and ads, shipping or overheads if you have 
them). Review the import, then see your true profit, a Health Score and the ranked fixes to make first. 
You can look at the Sample shop page first if you prefer. TikTok Shop UK Excel import is coming soon.
2. What exactly is needed to get started?
A verified account and a payment card to start the 14-day free trial — you pay nothing today. You 
upload reports you already download from your sales platforms. For Shopify, export the Orders CSV 
(the Shopify Payments transactions CSV is optional). For Amazon, download a Custom Unified 
Transaction CSV. For Etsy, download both the Orders CSV and the Order Items CSV (the Monthly 
Statement is optional). TikTok Shop UK (Beta): Excel import coming soon. You can also enter costs 
with the product CSV template or type them in. Buyer names, emails, addresses and similar personal 
details are removed at import. Your plan decides how many platforms you can use at once (Starter 1 
of 3, Growth up to 2 of 3, Pro and Scale all 3, while TikTok is coming soon).
3. How can I recommend a new feature I want to see?
Signed in, open Suggest a feature, write a short note and send it. It goes straight to the Margins365 
team. We can't promise a reply. Not signed in? Sign in first and you'll be taken straight to it. For 
anything else, use Contact us in the Help centre.
4. Can I cancel anytime?
Yes. Cancel any time in one click from Plan & billing — only the account holder (the owner) can do 
this. We don’t accept cancellation by email. If you cancel during your 14-day trial, you pay nothing. 
You keep access until your trial or paid period ends; then your account is closed and your data is 
permanently deleted. Download your data before then.
5. What happens after the 14-day trial?
A card is collected when you start, and you pay nothing today. Your trial gives you the plan you chose, 
with its features and order limit. Unless you cancel in Plan & billing before your trial ends (the date is 
shown at Checkout and in Plan & billing), that plan starts on day 15 at its price (or the founding price, 
if you have a founding place). We’ll email you a reminder before you’re charged. You can change plan 
during the trial; the trial end date stays the same. TikTok Shop UK Excel import is coming soon.
6. Is there a founding offer?
Yes, while places last. The first 100 paying customers get 50% off their first 6 monthly payments 
(Starter £19.50, Growth £39.50, Pro £64.50, Scale £99.50 a month including VAT), then the normal 
price. On an annual plan the first year is 25% off (Starter £292.50, Growth £592.50, Pro £967.50, 
Scale £1,492.50 including VAT), then the normal annual price. The number of places left shown on 
Pricing is the real count.
7. What are shops?
A shop is one online store connected to this account. If you sell from two Shopify stores, that is two 
shops. Starter, Growth and Pro include 1 shop. Scale includes up to 3. Unique orders count toward the 
account’s monthly limit. The monthly order limit is counted across all your shops together, not 
separately per shop.
8. What are seats?
A seat is a person who can sign in to this account. The owner always uses one seat. Extra seats are 
for people such as a bookkeeper or business partner. Starter and Growth include 1 seat (the owner). 
Pro includes 2. Scale includes 5. Only the account holder (the owner) can cancel the trial or 
subscription.
9. What happens if I hit my order limit?
Orders count once per billing month — re-uploading the same orders does not use your allowance 
again. We’ll warn you at 80% and name the next plan. If a file would take you over your limit, we 
won’t import part of it. You’ll see how many orders over you are and can upgrade. Upgrades take 
effect immediately with proration; re-upload after upgrading. Existing results stay visible. During a 
trial the limit is the limit of the plan you chose.
10. Is my data safe, and where is it stored?
Buyer names, emails, addresses and similar personal details are removed at import. The app, 
database and backups run on DigitalOcean in London. Transactional email is sent with Amazon SES in 
the EU (Stockholm). You can Download my data while your trial or subscription is active. If you cancel, 
you keep access until your trial or paid period ends; then your account is closed and your product 
data is permanently deleted. Backup copies are removed within 7 days. A GDPR / privacy contact 
address is in the privacy policy.
11. Do you connect to my store?
No. We never connect to your store, and no 3PL integration is required. You download the reports you 
already get from Shopify, Amazon or Etsy and upload them here.
12. Can I change plan?
Yes. You can upgrade at any time from Plan & billing. The upgrade takes effect immediately and we 
prorate the difference. After upgrading, re-upload if you were blocked by an order limit. During a trial 
you can change plan at any time; the trial end date stays the same and nothing is charged until day 
15. Downgrades on a paid plan apply at renewal; features, shops or seats beyond the lower plan are 
locked, not deleted, and come back when you upgrade.
13. Does the price include VAT?
Yes. All published prices and every Stripe price are tax-inclusive. Starter is £39 a month or £390 a 
year, Growth £79 / £790, Pro £129 / £1,290 and Scale £199 / £1,990, all including VAT. Founding 
prices include VAT too.
14. How do order limits work?
Each plan has a hard limit of unique orders per billing month: Starter 500, Growth 2,000, Pro 5,000 
and Scale 15,000. An order counts once per billing month, however many times you upload it, and the 
limit is shared across all your shops. On annual plans the limit still resets every month. During a trial 
the limit is the limit of the plan you chose. We warn you at 80% and name the next plan. A file that 
would take you over the limit is not imported (see “What happens if I hit my order limit?”).
15. What happens on annual plans?
You pay for 10 months and get 12: Starter £390, Growth £790, Pro £1,290 and Scale £1,990 a year, 
including VAT. With a founding place, the first year is 25% off (see “Is there a founding offer?”). 
Annual plans also start with the 14-day free trial of the plan you choose; unless you cancel in Plan & 
billing before the trial ends, the first annual payment is taken on day 15. Order limits still reset every 
month. We email you before each renewal. You can cancel any time in Plan & billing and keep access 
until the paid year ends; refunds follow the Refunds & cancellation policy.

12. The calculations
12.0 Definitions
• Revenue: product sales plus shipping charged, minus discounts, excluding VAT / sales tax / 
GST. Refunds are not deducted here; they are counted in return cost. 
• Product and packaging cost: unit cost + packaging cost, per unit sold. 
• Shipping cost: what the seller paid to ship orders (not what the customer paid). Excludes FBA 
orders, whose fulfilment fees count as fees. 
• Net shipping cost: shipping cost minus shipping charged (can be negative). 
• Shipping-eligible revenue: revenue from non-FBA physical orders. The Shipping part and 
the Shipping subsidy leak divide by this, not by total revenue. 
• Fees: marketplace, payment and fulfilment fees (actual where uploaded, otherwise 
"estimated" presets), mapped to the fee types in 12.3. 
• Order-level amounts split across lines (allocation): any amount given for a whole order 
rather than for each line (shipping charged, the seller's shipping cost per order, order-level 
fees including fixed per-order fees such as 20p, order-level discounts, order-level return costs 
and refunds) is split across the order's lines by line value (each line's share of the order's 
product revenue before order-level discounts). The split amounts are flagged "allocated" in 
"Show the maths" and add up exactly to the order amount (any rounding remainder goes to 
the last line). If every line in the order has zero value, the amount is split by units. A product's 
per-unit amount = the total allocated to its lines ÷ its units sold. Amounts the platform already 
gives per line are used as given. 
• Physical and digital orders: Shopify: a line is physical when its "Lineitem requires shipping" 
column is true; Amazon: always physical; Etsy: physical unless the user marks the listing (or 
the whole shop) as digital in settings. An order is physical if any line is physical. 
• Return cost: refunded amount + return shipping + return or restocking fees (from the 
platform files or the template).
• Refund allocation: where a refund is given for the whole order rather than per line, it 
is split across the order's lines by line value, using the allocation rule above, and 
flagged "allocated" in "Show the maths". 
• Contribution margin (CM): revenue − product and packaging cost − shipping cost − fees − 
return cost. CM % = CM ÷ revenue. 
• Net profit (true profit): CM − ad spend − currency conversion fees (Pro) − overheads (Pro, if 
entered). Net margin = net profit ÷ revenue. 
• Pro-rating to the period:
• Overheads are entered per month and are counted as monthly overheads × days in 
the period ÷ 30. 
• Ad spend entries cover their own date range; each entry counts only for the days that 
overlap the period (amount × overlapping days ÷ days in the entry's range). "Show the 
maths" shows the pro-rating. 
• Product net profit: the product's CM − its share of ad spend. Overheads and conversion fees 
are store-level only and are not spread across products. 
• Blended ROAS: revenue ÷ total ad spend for the period (all channels). Per-channel ROAS 
(Pro) = the attributed revenue the seller enters for that channel (from the ad platform's own 
report) ÷ that channel's ad spend. Where no attributed revenue is entered, only blended ROAS 
is shown. Per-channel ROAS is shown for information; the Health Score and leaks use blended 
ROAS. 
• Break-even ROAS: 1 ÷ CM %. If CM % is 0 or below, break-even ROAS is "not reachable". 
• Break-even ACOS: equal to CM % (ads can take up to this share of revenue before the store 
loses money). 
• Ad spend per product: total ad spend spread across products by their share of revenue. 
• Unit return rate: units refunded ÷ units sold.
• Estimated units refunded: where a file gives only a refund amount (for example 
Shopify's order-level refunds) and not the units refunded, units refunded for each line = 
the refund allocated to that line (refund allocation, above) ÷ the line's unit price, 
capped at the line's quantity. The result can be a fraction (for example 0.6 units) and is 
not rounded to whole units. Any unit return rate that uses estimated units is labelled 
"estimated from refund amount" in "Show the maths". It lowers the confidence level 
by one only when a High returns leak that is shown uses it (12.1).

Margins are rounded to 0.1% (decimal half-up) before the verdict is set.

Formulas
• Target price (before VAT/GST/sales tax) = (unit cost + packaging + per-unit shipping cost + 
per-unit ad cost + fixed per-order fees per unit) ÷ (1 − percentage fees − product return cost 
rate − target margin) − per-unit shipping charged.
• Why shipping charged is subtracted at the end: revenue includes shipping 
charged (12.0), and percentage fees, return cost and the target margin are all shares of 
that revenue. Solving on revenue and then taking off the shipping the customer already 
pays makes the product reach exactly the target margin. (Using net shipping cost in the 
top line instead would leave the margin short whenever the customer pays shipping: for 
example £18.67 gives 5.6% instead of 10%, while the formula above gives £20.00 and 
exactly 10%.) 
• Per-unit shipping cost = the product's shipping cost ÷ units sold; per-unit shipping 
charged = the shipping charged on its orders ÷ units sold (order-level shipping is split 
by line value, 12.0). 
• Where a platform's percentage fees don't apply to shipping charged, use: target price 
= (unit cost + packaging + per-unit shipping cost + per-unit ad cost + fixed per-order 
fees per unit − per-unit shipping charged × (1 − product return cost rate − target 
margin)) ÷ (1 − percentage fees − product return cost rate − target margin). 
• If the result is 0 or below (shipping charged alone covers the costs), the target price is 
shown as the lowest price, 0.01, with the note "Shipping charged already covers this 
product's costs". 
• Per-unit ad cost is held at its current amount (the product's current ad spend 
share ÷ units sold). Because ad spend is spread by revenue share, a higher price would 
otherwise pull more ad spend onto the product and the margin would fall short of the 
target. "Show the maths" states this assumption: "assumes ad spend on this product 
stays at £X per unit". 
• Product return cost rate = the product's return cost ÷ its revenue (store average if the 
product has fewer than 10 orders). 
• Where a platform charges percentage fees on the tax-inclusive price, percentage fees 
are multiplied by (1 + tax rate). 
• If the seller's prices include VAT/GST, the price shown to them = the unrounded target 
price × (1 + tax rate), then rounded up to the next 0.01. 
• Target margin defaults to 10% (the Healthy threshold); Subscribers can set their own. 
The Thin pricing leak uses the same target margin. 
• Already at target: if the target price is at or below the product's current average 
price, it is shown as "Current price already reaches your target margin (target price 
£X)", never as "raise price". 
• Products sold on more than one platform: each input is a blend of the product's 
own sales across its platforms, so one target price covers the current sales mix: 
percentage fees = the product's total percentage-based fees ÷ its total revenue (this 
weights each platform by revenue and allows for platforms that don't charge fees on 
shipping); fixed per-order fees, shipping cost and shipping charged per unit = the 
product's totals ÷ its total units sold (FBA units add no shipping cost); return cost rate = 
the product's total return cost ÷ its total revenue. "Show the maths" names the 
platform mix (for example "60% Shopify / 40% Amazon by revenue") and says it 
assumes the mix stays the same. A separate target price per platform comes later with 
the channel comparison (section 9). 
• The result is rounded up to the next 0.01 so the product reaches at least the target 
margin. 
• If the denominator is 0 or below, show "Can't reach the target margin at any 
price", with the cost share that causes it (fees, returns or the target margin). 
• Break-even price = target price with a 0% target margin. 
• Break-even ROAS = 1 ÷ CM %; break-even ACOS = CM % (see 12.0). 
• Rounding: all money and percentage calculations use decimal arithmetic (not floating 
point) with half-up rounding (0.05 rounds up, so 5.55% → 5.6%), except where a rule says 
"rounded up". Margins and percentages to 0.1%; money to 2 decimal places (whole units in 
summaries); scores to whole numbers. 
Scenario rules (Pro)
• A scenario changes price, product cost, ad spend, return rate, shipping charge or discount, for 
the whole store or for chosen products. 
• Units sold stay the same (no demand modelling); "Show the maths" says so. 
• When only price changes, ad spend per unit is held at its current amount (as in the 
target price), so a product priced at its target price shows the target margin. 
• A change in ad spend is spread across products by revenue share at the scenario's prices. 
• A return-rate change scales the product's return cost in proportion. 
• Everything else (fees, tax, Health Score, verdicts, leaks) is recalculated with the normal rules. 
• Approved preset table and how presets are applied: see 16.2. 
12.4 Uploads, plan history and hard monthly order limits
Approved by Akthar, 5 Nov 2026. This replaces every Free upload rule, “no monthly order

• An upload is one file or one complete multi-file platform set. Re-uploads merge using the 
canonical order/deduplication rules in 12.7. Sample data is not an upload and consumes no 
order allowance. 
• Plan history: Starter 3 months; Growth 12; Pro 24; Scale all imported history within fair use.

• Definitions: U is the set of canonical unique order IDs already counted in the current usage 
period. N is the set of canonical unique order IDs in the proposed whole upload that are not 
already in U. L is the active entitlement limit (the chosen plan’s limit, during the trial and after 
— K69; counted across all shops of the account — K68). Orders already seen on another day or 
in another file of the same period are not counted again. Multiple line items are one order;

new_unique = |N|

• Upgrade: immediate, with Stripe proration; the new limit and entitlement apply once Stripe 
confirms. The blocked file is not retained; the user re-uploads it. Downgrade of a paid plan is 
scheduled at next renewal, with the new limit/history effective then; existing results remain 
accessible subject to the new history window. A plan change during a trial applies immediately, 
keeps the trial end date and charges nothing today (K69). 
• Trial conversion and usage (K69): converting from a trial does not reset U. If a downgrade 
during the trial leaves projected above the new L, existing results stay visible and new unique-

conversion event itself is not a usage reset. 
• Failed payment: during the retry grace state the current plan remains active; after final 
failure it ends and no new uploads occur. After final failure, deletion follows 12.6; 
resubscription starts fresh and never restores deleted data. 
• Fair use: the separate 500,000 stored-order fair-use ceiling remains and is not a substitute for 
monthly hard limits. 
• Scale aggregation: 15,000 account-wide is the recommended default, not an approval (Q23). 
Keep aggregation scope configurable; do not silently bill or count per shop.

12.6 Data deletion and privacy at subscription end
K39 replaces the 12-month lapsed-retention rule, its 60/30-day warnings and restore-on-
resubscription. Trial conversion and paid downgrades are not access end and must not trigger this 
wipe.
• Scope of “all data wiped”: all uploaded/cleaned orders and raw files, product costs, 
advertising/overhead/return data, FX/tax/target settings, saved datasets/views, calculations, 
results, Health Scores, reports/PDFs, calculators/scenarios, samples attached to the account, 
shops, team/member links and other user preferences or product content. Deletion includes 
search indexes, caches, exports and derived data. 
• What may remain after access end: Stripe customer/subscription state held by Stripe, 
legally required invoices/payment/refund/dispute/tax records and a privacy-safe deletion 
tombstone/audit event. These are disclosed separately in Privacy/DPA and are not old product 
results. Stripe, Link, SES and Google retain data only under their disclosed roles/terms. After 
the owner cancels, the login itself is removed at access end (K51). There is no leftover account 
for a later resubscription. 
• Paid cancellation: access and Download my data continue to paid-period end. Before 
confirming, the screen states “Your subscription ends and your account will be closed on 
[exact date/time]. All your product data will be permanently deleted on that date. Download 
your data before then. This cannot be undone.” The confirmation email repeats the dates and 
export link. At period end, wipe all in-scope data and remove the login (K51). 
• Trial cancellation: no charge, through 12.5A. Access/export continue to the original trial end, 
then wipe all in-scope data and remove the login (K51). Cancellation screen/email show that 
exact account-closure and deletion date. 
• Final failed payment / immediate first-payment refund / lost dispute: access ends 
when the authoritative state says it ends; wipe immediately then. Retry/refund/dispute 
disclosures explain this before the event where possible, and a deletion confirmation is sent. 
No 12-month export-only period exists. 
• Later signup: after cancel-and-close, the previous login does not work. The person may 
create a new account; no old orders, results, costs, shops, team configuration or settings are 
restored. 
• Backup deletion: live data is wiped at the stated access end; all backups/replicas age out 
within 7 days. Configure backup retention to 7 days or less. Restore procedures re-apply 
deletion tombstones before access so wiped data never returns. 
• Warnings/jobs: no 60/30-day lapsed warnings. Only the applicable trial reminder, 
cancellation confirmation, final-failure/deletion notices and deletion-completion confirmation 
are sent. Scheduled jobs are idempotent and catch up missed deletion without extending 
access. 
• Privacy: Privacy policy, retention table, DPA/subprocessor list, Refunds & cancellation and 
Help must state that in-platform cancel also closes the account, the exact access-end and 
deletion warning, that a later signup is a new account, backup removal within 7 days, and 
exceptions for legally required payment records. They list a GDPR / privacy contact address 
only; they do not offer email cancellation or email account closure. Remove all 12-month 
retention/restoration promises and the former email-closure journey. 
• Account closure: no Delete account UI and no email-closure workflow (K51). The owner 
closes the account by cancelling in Plan & billing. At the disclosed access end the login is 
removed with the data. 
• Location: app/database/backups remain DigitalOcean London. Account/transactional emails 
use Amazon SES Stockholm (eu-north-1) and are processed in the EU (Stockholm), approved 
K36. Account emails are controller data and all processors appear in the DPA/subprocessor list. 
• Security: encryption in transit/at rest, least-privilege keys, signed webhooks, replay 
protection, rate limiting, secure cookies, CSRF protection, file validation and regular 
deletion/restore testing remain required.

13. Risks and assumptions
The risks in section 11 apply. Commercial assumptions are that sellers will pay top-end monthly prices 
after a card-required trial for ranked CSV/XLSX diagnosis, that samples plus a 14-day trial 
demonstrate value without a permanent free tier, and that hard order limits are acceptable while 
entitlement remains active. Technical assumptions are that real files match the documented 
mappings, Stripe supports the required Managed Payments subscription flow, Google brand 
verification succeeds, and the hosting/backup configuration can honour the deletion promises.
Risks carried from v17.1: column drift, translated headers, decimal/date formats, missing 
fee/tax/cost data, large-file memory limits on phones, in-app-browser Google restrictions, platform fee 
changes, currency-rate gaps, email delivery/spam, webhook ordering and disaster recovery. New 
subscription risks are entitlement drift, duplicate billing, unwanted trial conversion or repeat-trial 
abuse, annual-period usage errors, irreversible deletion at access end, accidental deletion at 
downgrade, unclear seat/multi-shop enforcement and unvalidated TikTok Beta calculations. Exact 
deletion dates, export prompts, tombstones and restore tests mitigate but do not remove these risks. 
Each has acceptance tests or an explicit owner/open question; none is solved by copy alone.

16. Build reference: imports, fees, tax, payments and sign-in
16.1 CSV and XLSX columns and mapping (build against these; confirm against real exports)
General parsing rules (all platforms; CSV and XLSX)
• Find each column by its header name, not by position. Ignore case and surrounding spaces, 
and treat a UTF-8 byte-order mark as part of the file, not the header. Columns that aren't 
recognised are ignored. Platforms add columns without notice: for example, Shopify's own help 
page says "Lineitem SKU", but real exports say "Lineitem sku", and Shopify Payments added 
"Payout ID" in January 2026. 
• If a required column is missing, the import fails and the message names the column. If an 
optional column is missing, the import continues and the dependent figure is labelled 
"estimated" or "not available" (section 6 rules). 
• Amounts can include currency signs and thousands separators, and some EU files use a 
comma as the decimal mark. Etsy statements write empty amounts as "--". Strip the symbols 
and parse using the file's locale. "--" means empty, not zero. 
• Dates come in several formats: text like "September 3, 2026" or "16 January, 2024", ISO dates 
with a time-zone offset, and Amazon's local formats. Parse all of them, and record the time 
zone if the file gives one. 
• Amazon's file starts with several description lines before the header row. Find the 
header row by looking for its first columns ("date/time", "settlement id"), not by assuming it is 
row 1. Amazon.de, .fr, .it and .es reports can have translated headers (for example 
"Datum/Uhrzeit", "Abrechnungsnummer", "Bestellnummer", "Umsätze", "Verkaufsgebühren"). 
The Etsy Monthly Statement can also be translated (German "Datum, Art, Titel..."). Keep one 
header dictionary per language, and reject an unknown language with a clear message. 
• Personal data columns are dropped at import, as section 6 requires. In the mapping tables 
below they are marked strip.

18. Deletion at subscription end and original files
• Active paid/trial and legacy lifetime: product data follows 12.6 and plan-history display 
rules. Trials have no special retention exception. 
• Ended subscription: at the disclosed trial/paid access end, all product/user data in 12.6 is 
wiped. After an owner cancel, the login is also removed (K51). Final payment failure and 
immediate first-payment refund wipe when access ends; the login is removed then as well, so 
there is no leftover empty account that would need an email-closure path. There is no 12-
month lapsed period and no restore-on-resubscription. Backups age out within 7 days. 
• Cancellation notices: paid/trial cancellation screen and email state the exact access-end, 
account-closure and deletion date and offer Download my data. A cancelled trial keeps access 
until the original trial end, not immediate deletion. 
• Original CSV/XLSX: deleted immediately on successful, failed or cancelled import; 
abandoned import cancels after 1 hour. Raw files are in temporary non-backed-up storage, 
never in exports, ordinary logs or support reports. Consensual TikTok fixtures are an exception 
with explicit consent and the recommended 30-day retention default (Q25). 
• Account closure: the owner cancels in Plan & billing; that also closes the account (K51). At 
the disclosed access end the login and product data are removed. No Delete account UI exists 
and there is no email-closure workflow. The privacy policy lists a GDPR contact address only. 
• Start fresh: immediate app deletion and backup ageing within 7 days; preserves subscription 
and shop/team configuration. It is not a substitute for cancellation or closure. 
• Restore safety: disaster-recovery restore re-applies deletion tombstones and removes 
raw/deleted data before user access. Deleted subscription data never returns. 
• Acceptance: D1–D12 plus raw-file deletion in every import outcome. No old lapsed-retention 
clock/warning/restore flow remains.

19.1 Seven-day deletion promise
Account closure, Start fresh, subscription/trial-end deletion, final-failure/refund deletion, raw files 
captured exceptionally and any user deletion leave live storage and all backups within 7 days. Backup 
retention is 7 days or less. Deleted product data is never restored; legally required billing/tax records 
and privacy-safe deletion tombstones follow their separately disclosed retention rules.

backups. Original CSV/XLSX files need no persistent storage. Existing £ cost estimates are 
informational, not repriced in v18. Legal review of DigitalOcean's DPA remains open.
19.3 Scheduled jobs and monitoring
• Every 15 minutes: cancel imports abandoned for 1 hour and delete raw files; process due 
trial/paid/final-failure/refund deletion jobs; apply deletion tombstones and backup-retention 
tasks. Trial reminders and cancellation/deletion confirmations use scheduled jobs with delivery 
alerts. 
• Daily: Stripe entitlement reconciliation; backup/restore checks; subscription/invoice 
consistency; email delivery and webhook failure alerts. 
• Deletion cut-offs are enforced by access rules even if a job is late. Missed jobs catch up without 
skipping; warning/deletion timestamps are recorded in the user's account time zone and UTC 
internally. 
• Error logging uses the approved DigitalOcean logs/alerts baseline or a separately approved 
tool with UK/EU processing and no personal data. Secrets, authorization headers and file 
contents are never logged. 
19.4 Acceptance
1. App/database/backups are LON1; production managed database has 7-day or shorter backup 
retention. 
2. Abandoned import file deleted by 1 hour plus the 15-minute job interval; no Free deletion timer 
exists. 
3. Trial/paid deletion and reminder/confirmation jobs survive a missed run and catch up correctly; 
a cancelled trial never converts because a job was late. 
4. Restore test removes raw/deleted subscription data before user access; a later signup cannot 
recover it. 
5. Account closure, Start fresh and ended-subscription data are absent from backups older than 7 
days; privacy/DPA wording and deletion scope match.

23.2 Shops and multi-shop (K68)
Entity. Shop: id, account, name (1–60 characters, unique in the account), platforms enabled (within 
tier slots), currency, created, status active or deleting. Every order, cost, ad, overhead, saved dataset, 
scenario, result and upload belongs to exactly one shop. Settings that are per account: plan, billing, 
team, time zone. Settings that are per shop: platforms, currency, tax settings, target margin, FX 
settings.
Tier limits. Starter 1, Growth 1, Pro 1, Scale up to 3 (trial or paid). The monthly unique-order limit is 
account-wide on every plan (one bucket across all shops). Platform slots apply per shop (Starter 1, 
Growth up to 2, Pro and Scale all 3, while TikTok is coming soon).
Flows.
1. First shop. Created in first-run (23.5): name and platform. Required before upload. 
2. Add shop (Scale, owner). Account menu → Add shop, or Shops → Add shop → name, 
platforms → Create → the new shop opens empty with its own first-run upload prompt. At the 
limit the button is replaced by “Scale includes up to 3 shops” (no upgrade beyond Scale). 
3. Add shop on Starter, Growth or Pro (owner). The Add shop action is shown and opens an 
upgrade explainer: “Add up to 3 shops on Scale (£199/mo).” It never creates a second shop 
below Scale. 
4. Rename (owner). Inline; takes effect everywhere at once. 
5. Switch (Scale, everyone). From the account-button switcher or Shops. Switching changes 
the current shop in the address, keeps the same page type (for example Leaks → Leaks), and 
opens at the top. 
6. Remove (owner, Scale, when more than one shop). Shops → Remove → confirmation 
states the shop name, the item counts that will be deleted and “This can't be undone”; the 
owner types the shop name to confirm. The shop's product data is deleted (12.6 scope, 
backups within 7 days). Order usage already counted this billing month is not refunded. The 
last remaining shop cannot be removed; Start fresh (17.3) empties it instead. 
7. Combined view (Scale). The Control room offers “All shops” as a read-only combined 
summary: true profit, revenue and leak £ per shop side by side and in total. The Health Score 
stays per shop (it is not averaged), so formulas are unchanged. 
Downgrade from Scale. Applies at renewal (Q27). Before it applies, the owner chooses which one 
shop stays active. Other shops become locked (kept, not visible, not uploadable) and unlock on 
upgrade. If the owner does not choose, the oldest shop stays active. Locked shops are deleted only 
when access ends (K39).
Errors and edge cases. Duplicate name → inline error. Upload while a shop is being removed → 
refused with a clear message. A member removed mid-session loses access on next request. Two 
owners' tabs removing the same shop → second gets “This shop has already been removed”.
Acceptance. SH-1 Starter/Growth/Pro cannot create a second shop by any route (UI or API). SH-2 
Scale can create, rename, switch and remove up to 3 shops. SH-3 Data never appears in another shop 
(orders, costs, results, exports, PDF, search). SH-4 The order limit is counted once across all shops; re-
uploading the same orders to the same shop does not add usage. SH-5 Removing a shop deletes its 
data and keeps usage; the last shop cannot be removed. SH-6 Switching survives refresh. SH-7 Scale 
downgrade locks, not deletes, extra shops; upgrade restores them.

1. Server cancel (owner only). Confirm cancel calls one server route, for example POST

Stripe and checks: cancel_at_period_end is true (or the status is canceled), and Stripe's upcoming-invoice 
preview returns no invoice to be paid. For a trial, this is the proof that the card will not be charged. 
Only then does it record the cancellation and answer success. It stores the Stripe subscription ID, 
cancel_at, the check time and the result.

in the app and a pop-up says: “We couldn’t cancel your [trial/subscription]. Nothing has changed — 
you will still be charged on [date] unless you cancel. Please try again.” with Try again and Close.

email].” 
• Body (paid): “Your [Plan] subscription is cancelled. No further payments will be taken. You 
keep access until [date, time, time zone]. …” then the same last sentences. 
• Buttons: Download my data and Close.

• Subject (trial): “Your Margins365 trial is cancelled — you won’t be charged”. Paid: “Your 
Margins365 subscription is cancelled”. 
• Body includes: cancellation reference; plan (and monthly or annual); date and time cancelled; 
access until (date, time, time zone); “No further charges will be taken” (trial: “You will not be

the server retries and alerts the admin.

cancelled”). It reads the server record, not the browser, so it is the same on every device and after 
signing out and in. The Cancel button is replaced by Resubscribe.
7. Resubscribe.
• Before access ends: Resubscribe (“Keep my plan”) asks for one confirmation stating the 
charge date and amount, then sets cancel_at_period_end: false in Stripe. The trial or period 
continues with the same end date. It does not start a new trial. A pop-up and an email confirm 
it. 
• After access ends: the account is closed (K51). The person can start again with a new account 
through Checkout; a second free trial is not given (one trial per account, and per card by 
default, as in 12.5 and K40). 
8. Audit log. Every attempt writes one entry: time, account ID, user, role, plan, Stripe subscription 
ID, action (requested, confirmed, failed, resubscribed), cancel_at, the result of the step 2 check, 
reference, and IP. Only admins can read it. It is kept with the billing records (23.13).
9. Stripe-side changes stay in sync. The webhook (customer.subscription.updated and deleted) 
applies the same state when the subscription is cancelled or resumed in Stripe or Link: it records the 
cancellation, sends the same email once and shows the same status. When the subscription ends, the 
account closes and the data wipe runs (K39/K51). It must not set a “free” plan (G9; the Free plan was 
retired).
Acceptance. CX-1 Confirm cancel changes the Stripe subscription (cancel_at_period_end true or 
canceled) with no upcoming invoice. CX-2 The pop-up appears only after that check passes. CX-3 One 
email with every field above. CX-4 Plan & billing shows “Cancelled — access until …” and the Stripe 
line on any device. CX-5 A failure shows the failure pop-up and changes nothing. CX-6 Resubscribe
